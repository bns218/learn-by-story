# STUDY NOTES RULE (updated 2026-10-30, supersedes the STUDY NOTES RULE in pipeline.md)

**RESEARCH FIRST: follow claude/research.md before writing. Every fact, date and number must be checked against reliable sources, and EVERY study-notes doc ends with a "Sources and further reading" section (4-8 linked sources actually opened in the run) plus a one-line accuracy note (see research.md).**

**LENGTH: every study-notes doc must be MORE THAN 3 PAGES: aim for 4-6 pages and at least about 2,000 words (user requirement). Count words before finishing; if it is shorter, add more worked examples, more definitions, more "what the video skipped" material and more self-check questions.**

**STUDY NOTES RULE (user requirement, updated): for every concept, also write a DETAILED study-notes document ("notebook") that teaches the concept fully to students and new learners, to go with the LinkedIn post.** NOT a Jupyter notebook. The video is the short story; the notes are the complete lesson, so they MUST cover everything about the concept, including sub-topics, terms and ideas the video did not have time to explain. One Docs doc per concept (create with the Docs tools, title `<id> <concept>: study notes`, with the line "Learn AI by Story" under the title), plain-language and beginner friendly, 4-6 pages. Sections: (1) the idea in one sentence; (2) the story from the video in 3-4 lines; (3) KEY DEFINITIONS: a glossary of every term, each with a precise definition and a one-line plain-English meaning, including terms not used in the video; (4) detailed step-by-step explanation, building from basics to the full picture, with the "why" behind each step; (5) everything the video skipped: related concepts, variants and types, history/background, maths or formulas in simple words where relevant, limitations and trade-offs, how it connects to earlier and later roadmap concepts (reference roadmap IDs); (6) at least TWO worked examples with real numbers, step by step (verify the arithmetic in code); (7) comparison tables or simple diagrams where they help; (8) common mistakes and myths; (9) real-life uses with concrete examples; (10) 8-10 self-check questions with answers (mix of definitions, why-questions and small problems); (11) key takeaways, a "go deeper" list (what to read or learn next), and the next roadmap ID; (12) Sources and further reading, plus the accuracy note. Mark illustrative figures as illustrative. A short code snippet only if it truly helps. Open each doc with the Artifact open action and list the doc links in the final message. The LinkedIn text post for each video mentions the notes ("Full study notes: link in comments") and the best 2-3 source links.
